what_i_learned: |
  Today I wrapped up a 1500-episode DDQN training run using real patient data and static features. The model converged smoothly, achieving a consistent max reward of 24 across all episodes with a 99.87% convergence rate. I ran fairness evaluations across patient age groups, showing nearly identical glucose control accuracy (TIR) and a Disparate Impact ratio of 1.0. I also reviewed TIR, TBR, and TAR metrics across multiple health parameters like creatinine, insulin, and triglycerides — confirming the model’s robustness across key markers.

blockers: |
  While the model performed consistently well in glucose management, metrics for other variables like HbA1c and total cholesterol revealed edge-case issues (e.g., 100% below range). These will need attention in future iterations or sensitivity experiments.

reflection: |
  Seeing my DDQN model reach full convergence and fairness alignment today was a milestone. It proves that with careful tuning and structure, reinforcement learning can achieve both performance and equity in real clinical datasets. Now I’m even more motivated to push into transformer-based hybrids and more dynamic reward shaping.
